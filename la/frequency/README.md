# Latin word-form frequency list (`la/frequency/`)

Our own counts on public-domain Latin texts (Heinrich `docs/LATIN.md` L-D5), made with the shared tokenizer of
the other lists (`tools/frequency/tok.py`) and the Latin count script `tools/frequency/count_la.py`. Texts were
read in memory and discarded; only token counts are stored. No third-party word list, frequency list, lexicon
or dictionary was used (no Perseus/Morpheus, LASLA, Whitaker's Words, Collatinus, Wiktionary, Lewis & Short,
Georges or similar), and no text from Perseus, The Latin Library, Corpus Corporum or the PHI Latin texts.
Counted on 2026-10-21.

## Files
- `forms.tsv` - case-sensitive, normalised word forms with count >= 3, sorted by total count. Columns:
  `form`, `total`, `prose`, `poetry`, `late`, `sentence_initial` (`sentence_initial` stays column 6, as in the
  other languages).
- `STATS.md` - tokens per group, coverage curve, top 50 forms (generated by `merge.py`; "files" there are
  authors).
- `works.tsv` - every work counted: group, author (as on Wikisource), work, source, pages, tokens.

The authors of each group are listed in `tools/frequency/sources_la.py` (bibliographic knowledge only). Texts
written after 1500 (Neo-Latin, papal documents, modern Latin, Latin translations made after 1500) are not
counted. In `late` every author except the Vulgate is capped at 60,000 tokens (works in a fixed pseudo-random
order, cut at a paragraph), so that a few prolific writers do not dominate; without the cap Augustine alone
would be larger than all classical prose. `late` is still larger than `prose` and `poetry` together: for school
Latin, weight the columns (for example `prose` + `poetry` alone) rather than using `total` as it is.

## Source checks (L-D5)
- A Wikisource page is **not counted** when its own header (`Fons`, `Editio`) or text, or the header of a parent
  page, names Perseus, The Latin Library, Corpus Corporum (mlat.uzh.ch) or the PHI Latin texts - as a link, a
  source field or a credit line copied with the text. This removed 2,785 pages of the selected authors, among
  them Livy books I-X and XXI-XXII (Perseus), Vergil's *Aeneis*, Ovid's *Amores*, Eutropius, most of Plautus
  (The Latin Library), Cicero's *Pro Ligario* (Perseus), the *Rhetorica ad Herennium*, Seneca's *De brevitate
  vitae* and *Agamemnon*, and nearly all Patrologia Latina texts imported from Corpus Corporum.
- Pages without a named source edition: public-domain works, edition unknown, counted; pages naming Perseus,
  The Latin Library, Corpus Corporum or PHI excluded (2,785 pages). Many older Wikisource pages name no source;
  a transcription of a public-domain text carries no new rights, and only counts are kept.
- Only the Latin main text is counted: Wikisource headers, navigation, tables, lists of contents, headings,
  footnotes (`<ref>`), sections such as `Notae`, `Adnotationes`, `Apparatus`, sub-pages written by an editor
  (`Monitum`, `Admonitio`, `Prolegomena`, `Notitia`, `Index`, `Praefatio editoris` ...), translations and
  interlinear or annotated school editions are left out (`EXCLUDE_WORKS` in `sources_la.py`; for example the
  interlinear Latin-English Nepos and a French Pliny). Paragraphs with function words of English, German,
  French, Italian or Spanish (`FOREIGN` in `count_la.py`) are dropped as a second safeguard.
- The Clementine Vulgate (1592) is counted, not the pages that follow a modern critical text of the Vulgate.

## Normalisation (L-D2)
- **Diacritics removed**: macrons, breves, diaereses and the accents of old prints (`Rōmā`, `poëta`, `quàm`,
  `Musâ` -> `Roma`, `poeta`, `quam`, `Musa`); `æ`/`œ` -> `ae`/`oe` (`Æ` -> `Ae`, in a word in capitals `AE`); long `ſ`
  and the `ß` of early prints -> `s`/`ss`.
- **u/v and i/j folded**: every `v` becomes `u` and every `j` becomes `i`, also in capitals: `vir` -> `uir`,
  `Julius` -> `Iulius`, `Vergilius` -> `Uergilius`. This is a mechanical key, the same for every edition
  (whether it prints `v` or `u`, `j` or `i`); to look a form up, fold it the same way. Showing the school
  spelling (`v` for consonantal u) is the lexicon's job.
- **Case is kept**, as in the other languages: `Et` and `et` are separate forms; `sentence_initial` counts how
  often a form opens a sentence. A line break also opens a sentence, so in verse every line start counts
  (many editions capitalise the first word of each verse; most here do not).
- **Enclitics are not split off.** The list counts word forms as they stand in the text: `uirumque`,
  `Romaeque`, `estne`, `mecum`, `quibuscum` are one form each; `-que`, `-ne`, `-ue` written as separate tokens
  occur only where an edition hyphenates them. Splitting is the engine tokenizer's job (LATIN.md L0).
- A token is a run of letters; hyphens, apostrophes, brackets and punctuation separate tokens (`ampliu'` ->
  `ampliu`, `quoi(u)s` -> `quoi`, `u`, `s`).

## Filters
- Anything touching a digit is dropped (verse, chapter and page numbers); a bracket or parenthesis holding a digit
  is removed as a reference (`(Ioan. 14,28)`, `[Schol. Gron. p. 436]`).
- Words written entirely in capitals (two or more letters) are not counted: headings, running heads, speaker
  labels in drama (`CH.`, `PA.`), Roman numerals (`XIV`), editors' sigla, and the capitalised first word of a
  book in some prints (`ARMA virumque`). The capitals `I`, `V`, `X` standing alone (numerals) are not counted
  either; `C`, `M`, `L`, `P`, `Q` ... are kept, mostly abbreviated praenomina (`C. Iulius`, `M. Tullius`).
- Short lines that hold a number (`Caput XII.`, `Liber II`, `Carmen 5`, `Capitulum I`) are headings and are
  dropped.
- Greek words have no Latin letters and produce no tokens; Wiki markup (templates, links, tags, tables) is
  stripped before counting, HTML entities are unescaped twice.
- The sentence splitter knows Latin abbreviations (praenomina `Cn.`, `Sex.`, `Ti.`, single letters, dates
  `Kal.`, `Non.`, `Id.`, offices `cos.`, `tr. pl.` ...; `ABBR_LA` in `tok.py`).

## Known limitations
- Small corpus (9.5M tokens; French has 151M), so the tail is thin: 60 % of the distinct forms occur once or
  twice, and the 164,964 kept forms cover 96.8 % of the tokens. Latin inflection spreads every lemma over many
  forms, so even common lemmas have rare forms.
- Some school authors are thin or missing because their Wikisource pages come from a source we do not use:
  Livy books I-X and XXI-XXII (only 141 scan pages of book I came in), Plautus (806 tokens), Ovid's *Amores*,
  Eutropius.
- Editions differ in spelling (`uolt`/`uult`, `optumus`/`optimus`, `inmitto`/`immitto`, `quom`/`cum`,
  medieval `e` for `ae`, `michi`); forms are counted as printed. Medieval spellings occur mainly in `late`.
- Wikisource transcriptions without a named source are counted as they are; their typos and OCR errors stay in
  the tail.
- Words with a lost letter in early prints (`perÐ` and similar) and Danish names in Saxo Grammaticus (`Høtherus`)
  appear in the tail.
